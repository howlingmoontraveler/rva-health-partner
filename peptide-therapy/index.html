Most peptide clinics in Richmond hand you a menu, run a quick consult, and hand you a syringe. We start with a body composition scan, bloodwork, and an independent physician evaluation through Asher Health — then decide, with data, whether a peptide protocol is actually appropriate.

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that signal specific processes in the body: tissue repair, fat metabolism, growth hormone release, cellular repair. Some, like semaglutide and tirzepatide, are FDA-approved medications. Others, like BPC-157, GHK-Cu, and sermorelin, are not FDA-approved drugs but are used in physician-directed clinical protocols.

This page covers what we coordinate access to, how the process actually works, how it compares to a typical Richmond peptide clinic, and the questions clients ask most before applying.

How peptide protocols work here

Peptides are never the starting point. Data is. Every protocol — if one is appropriate at all — follows the same four steps.

1

Baseline testing

Body composition scan, resting metabolic rate test, and bloodwork establish where you actually stand.

2

Physician evaluation

Licensed physicians through Asher Health independently review your history, labs, and goals to determine what's appropriate.

3

Integrated protocol

If a peptide is appropriate, it's built into your strength training and nutrition program, not handed to you standalone.

4

Monitoring & rescans

Scheduled rescans and check-ins track whether the protocol is producing measurable change, and the plan adjusts accordingly.

All clinical decisions — whether a peptide is appropriate, dosing, and prescribing — are made independently by the licensed physicians at Asher Health. RVA Health Partner does not practice medicine, prescribe medication, or make clinical decisions.
